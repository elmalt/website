Working Papers

Hospitals, Insurers, and the Variation in Negotiated Prices: Evidence from Transparency in Coverage Data

October 2026

Why does the same procedure cost several times more at one hospital than at another? Using the 2024 Transparency in Coverage files of three of the largest U.S. commercial insurers, UnitedHealthcare, the Blue Cross Blue Shield plans and Cigna, which together hold roughly 40% of the commercial market, I measure 1.7 million negotiated facility prices for six common services at 4,142 U.S. hospitals. A hip or knee replacement costs $27,157 at the median, about twice what Medicare pays, and prices for the same service vary four- to six-fold across hospitals. A bias-corrected two-way fixed effects (AKM) decomposition shows that the hospital, not the insurer, drives this variation: hospitals explain 53% of the variance in log prices and insurers 11%. Within an insurer, a hospital is paid essentially one price: different networks and self-funded versus fully insured plans differ by about 3%. High-price hospitals belong to systems, are large, and are located in concentrated, high-income markets; rural hospitals are paid 14 to 24% less.

With Deniz Igan and Divya Kirti
